# Help resources and freedom indexes

Resource links checked: 2026-10-26.

This directory brings together NGOs offering useful information or
assistance, and country-level assessments of internet freedom,
journalist safety, and freedom of expression. These resources can later
be split into separate pages.

## NGOs: information and practical assistance

### Access Now

- Website and help: <https://www.accessnow.org/help/>
- Service details: <https://www.accessnow.org/help/helpline-services/>
- Eligibility and FAQ: <https://www.accessnow.org/help/helpline-faq/>

Access Now's Digital Security Helpline provides free, round-the-clock
technical assistance to civil society around the world. It supports
activists, journalists, human rights defenders, and organizations facing
digital security problems.

Use it for direct digital security help and practical guidance. Consult
the service details and eligibility information before requesting help.

### Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF)

- Surveillance Self-Defense: <https://ssd.eff.org/>
- Introductory guides: <https://ssd.eff.org/module-categories/basics>

EFF's Surveillance Self-Defense guides explain privacy, secure
communication, risk assessment, and protection against surveillance.
They are useful for ordinary users as well as activists and journalists.

This entry is a self-help information resource, rather than a promise of
individual emergency assistance.

### Committee to Protect Journalists (CPJ)

- Assistance: <https://cpj.org/journalist-safety-and-emergencies/>
- Safety Kit: <https://cpj.org/safety-kit/>

CPJ provides safety information and short-term emergency support to
eligible journalists and media workers following incidents connected
with their journalistic work. Support may include medical, legal,
psychosocial, relocation, and other assistance.

Its Safety Kit covers physical, digital, and psychological safety.
The assistance page explains eligibility and the limits of its support.

### Reporters Without Borders (RSF)

- Assistance:
  <https://rsf.org/en/assistance-journalists-and-media>
- Country index: <https://rsf.org/en/index>

RSF assists journalists and media facing threats. Its resources include
support for critical situations and training in physical, psychological,
and digital security.

Use the assistance page for current application and contact details.
Its country index is described separately below.

### Front Line Defenders

- Organization and support:
  <https://frontlinedefenders.org/en/about-us>

Front Line Defenders supports human rights defenders at risk, including
emergency support for those in immediate danger and international
advocacy on their behalf.

Its work focuses on human rights defenders. Consult its current support
information to determine whether a request fits its remit.

### Amnesty International

- Annual report, 2026:
  <https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/pol10/0320/2026/en/>

Amnesty International publishes country reports and research on human
rights conditions. Its 2026 annual report documents developments during
2025 in 144 countries.

This is a source of country information and human rights research. The
linked report is not an emergency assistance service or a country ranking.

## Country-level freedom and safety assessments

These publishers measure different things. A country score, a ranking,
and a count of documented incidents should be presented separately.
Always show the publisher and edition or reporting period alongside data.

### Internet freedom: Freedom House

- Freedom on the Net:
  <https://freedomhouse.org/report/freedom-net>
- Research methodology:
  <https://freedomhouse.org/pl/node/349>

Freedom on the Net provides country scores and reports covering:

- Obstacles to internet access.
- Restrictions on online content.
- Violations of internet users' rights.

It is a useful starting point for comparing censorship and online rights.
Coverage is selective rather than worldwide. Display an unlisted country
as **not assessed**, rather than assigning it a score or assuming freedom.

### Press freedom: RSF World Press Freedom Index

- Country map and list: <https://rsf.org/en/index>
- Methodology, 2026:
  <https://rsf.org/en/methodology-used-compiling-world-press-freedom-index-2026>

The 2026 index covers 180 countries and territories. It assesses the
political, legal, economic, social, and security conditions for journalism.

The overall score measures press freedom, including journalist safety.
It should not be labeled simply as a personal safety score. Keep the
published score, rank, and edition together when displaying results.

### Journalist safety: CPJ's database of attacks on the press

- Database: <https://cpj.org/data/>

CPJ provides country-filterable records of journalists killed, imprisoned,
or missing. These records help users examine documented incidents and
the people affected.

This is an incident database, not a general country safety ranking.
Retain the selected dates, category, and CPJ's definitions when presenting
counts. Low recorded counts alone do not establish that a country is safe
for journalists.

### Freedom of expression: ARTICLE 19

- Global Expression Report: <https://www.globalexpressionreport.org/>
- About the report: <https://www.globalexpressionreport.org/about>
- Methodology: <https://www.globalexpressionreport.org/methodology>

The Global Expression Report assesses people's ability to express
themselves, communicate, and access information. Its methodology combines
25 indicators into a country-level measure.

Its scope extends beyond professional journalism. Use the edition and
coverage stated in the underlying dataset when displaying country scores;
the publication year and the year being assessed may differ.

### Civic freedoms: CIVICUS Monitor

- Country reports and map: <https://monitor.civicus.org/>
- Country data: <https://monitor.civicus.org/data/>
- Rating definitions:
  <https://monitor.civicus.org/about/how-it-works/ratings/>

CIVICUS Monitor assesses civic space: the conditions in which people can
speak, organize, and protest. Country updates explain developments behind
the assessments.

Its rating categories are:

- Open.
- Narrowed.
- Obstructed.
- Repressed.
- Closed.

These are civic-space assessments, rather than internet-only or
journalist-only rankings.

## Notes for future website integration

Keep NGO assistance and country assessments distinct when splitting this
page. A possible structure is an NGO directory at `/resources/` and a
country browser at `/freedom/`, with links from Dangerous Map.

For NGO entries, retain:

- Organization name and official resource link.
- What help or information it provides.
- Intended users and assistance eligibility.
- Whether it offers self-help material or direct assistance.
- Date the resource was last checked.

For country assessments, retain:

- Publisher and original source link.
- Metric name and explanation.
- Edition, reporting period, and date retrieved.
- Published score, rank, or category, with its scale explained.
- Missing-data or not-assessed status.

Keep the different assessments separate rather than combining them into
one SOS Relay danger score. Link users to the original country reports
for context. Before copying datasets or republishing graphics, check each
publisher's current reuse and attribution terms.
