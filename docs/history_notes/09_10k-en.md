# Cuneiform Script

Cuneiform script was one of the earliest systems of writing, first developed by ancient Sumerians in Mesopotamia around 3400–3100 BC. It is named after the Latin word "cūnēus," meaning "楔形" (wedge-shaped), due to its distinctive wedge-like marks made on clay tablets with a reed stylus.

The script was used by many cultures over thousands of years, including the Akkadians, Babylonians, Assyrians, Elamites, Hittites, Luwians, Persians, and others. Cuneiform documents have been found in various parts of the Middle East, as far west as Syria and Cyprus.

Cuneiform writing was primarily a system of logograms, each symbol representing a word or phrase. However, it also included syllabary signs that stood for sounds. The script underwent many changes over time, evolving from early Sumerian to Old Akkadian and then to the more complex Neo-Assyrian and Babylonian scripts.

The discovery and decipherment of cuneiform have greatly enhanced our understanding of ancient Mesopotamian civilizations, providing invaluable insights into their history, culture, religion, and daily life.
